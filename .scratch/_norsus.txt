 Analyzing data variability in EPDs of crushed stone and asphalt  
Dat e: 12. september 2025  
Report number : AR 11.25 
Authors : Bojana Petrovic, Hanne Lerche  Raadal  
 
 
Introduction  
Demand  for EPDs is relatively high nowadays in the construction sector (Konradsen, et al. 
2024) . The growing trend of EPD comparisons emphasizes the need for construction material 
stakeholders to adopt a more harmonized  methodology to ensure consistent and reliable 
assessments across different materials (AzariJafari, et al. 2021) , (Bribián, Capilla og Usón 
2011) . It is important to note that EPDs are developed in accordance with product category 
rules (PCRs) (Konradsen, et al. 2024) . However, there is a risk of inconsistency and possible 
rule  conflicts that could influence the compatibility of EPDs since different programme 
operators  publish PCRs . Thus, using an EPD of a specific product can present significantly 
different impact results when following one or another guideline . It is stated that the EPDs are 
not highly accurate and comparable, highlighting the issue with the harmonization of the EPD 
system (Konradsen, et al. 2024) .  

 
Norwegian Institute for Sustaina bility Research   Page 2 of 5 The main objective of this report is to analyze various EPDs for crushed stone and asphalt by 
comparing their GWP (fossil emissions ) for the production phase (A1 -A3). The analysis focuses 
on how emissions are reported across various crushing stages, highlighting any variations in 
the way these emissions are declared for different product types and producers. By comparing 
these emissions, the report aims to discover  potential d eviations  and inconsistencies in the 
reporting process, which are essential for making accurate, informed decisions regarding the 
declared  emissions  of these products . 
Analysis of EPDs for crushed stone  
In an EPD for crushed stone, different crushing stages are directly linked to the type of crushed 
stone product being produced, as the number of crushing stages influences both the physical 
properties of the final product (e.g., size and quality) and its e nvironmental impact. The EPD 
ensures transparency by detailing how these stages vary for each product type, enabling 
stakeholders to make informed decisions about the environmental footprint of each product . 
The complexity of emissions declared in EPDs for crushed stone underscores the need for 
careful interpretation.  
The assessment include s comparison s between different crushing stages across 30 EPDs of 
crushed stone from the Norwegian market. All the EPDs are published by EPD -Norway , a 
program operator for Environmental Product Declaration (EPD) type III according to ISO 14025.

Norwegian Institute for Sustaina bility Research   Page 3 of 5 During the production of different types of crushed stone products, emissions are categorized 
into specific crushing stages, commonly referred to as Stage 0, 1, 2, 3, and , in some cases 
Stage 4. These crushing stages are not necessarily harmonized across all EPDs. Different EPDs 
may group , combine , define , and include stages differently. Therefore, when analyzing EPDs 
for crushed stone, it is essential to interpret how each crushing stage is reported carefully  and 
for which products the stages are representative to ensure alignment with the intended 
application or comparison criteria.   
Variability in declared emissions across stages involves differences in the number of crushing 
steps required for various product types. Products requiring additional refinement or finer 
granularity naturally  have higher emissions. Differences in emissions may also stem from 
variations in product -specific requirements (e.g., size, application).  
The variations in declared fossil GWP for modules A1-A3 for the included  crushing stages  in the  
30 EPDs are presented below :  
• Stage 0, which represents the initial phase of raw material extraction, has the lowest 
variation in emissions, with a minimum of 0.4 kg CO 2e/ton and a maximum of 3.4 kg 
CO2e/ton, and an average of 1.5 kg CO 2e/ton.  
• Stage 1 presents emissions, ranging from 0.7 to 4.5 kg CO 2e/ton, with an average of 2.3 
kg CO 2e/ton.  
• Stage 2 has the highest emissions variability, with values spanning from 1.7 to 5.9 kg 
CO2e/ton and an average of 3.1 kg CO 2e/ton.  
• Similarly, Stage 3 emissions range from 1.8 to 4.6 kg CO 2e/ton, with an average of 2.9 kg 
CO2e/ton, suggesting a consistent energy demand for the processes in this stage, 
despite some differences between producers.  
In addition, approximately 9 EPDs  were found to have larger deviations , which need to be 
further investigated . For example, t he EPD ( NEPD -4273 -3505 -NO) shows lower emissions in 
crushing stages 3 -4 compared to crushing stages 1 -2, which should not be possible  since 
emissions from previous steps shall be added to the next  stage . This seems to be the current 
practice, even though it is not explicitly described in either the PCR or the EPDs . Further more , 
the EPDs ( NEPD -4189 -3413 -NO, NEPD -5770 -5054 -NO) combine two crushing stages and 
declare a  one value . The following  EPDs have added an additional stage that usually  describe s 
washing (“vaskeverk ”), but also combined  crushing stages  (NEPD -5188 -4496 -NO; NEPD -
5919 -5190 -NO; NEPD -4189 -3413 -NO). The EPDs ( NEPD -5898 -5170 -NO; NEPD -5185 -4499 -
NO and  NEPD -4200 -3429 -NO include descriptions of declared stages that differ from standard 
practices, and certain stages are declared without explicitly referring to the corresponding 
stages.

Additionally, some EPDs were tested using the LCA software One Click LCA as part of a quality 
check . It was observed that the majority of EPDs include declared emissions separated into 
the different crushing stages. It is, however, hard to compare the different products /qualities  
because they do not necessarily  require  the same number of crushing stages  across the 
producers.  
Analysis of EPDs for asphalt  
When comparing asphalt products with similar characteristics, it is essential to account for 
variations in material composition. For example:  
• Group 1: 9 EPDs from Nord Vei & Asfalt AS, Brani AS and Velde Produksjon AS, with 
similar characteristics (containing 5 –6% bitumen and 94 –95% aggregate), the GWP (A1 –
A3) fossil emissions range from 17.9 to 83.1 kg CO₂e/ton.  
• Group 2: 7 EPDs from Skanska Industrial Solutions AB for hot mix asphalt used in road 
construction  with around 40% reclaimed asphalt included, fossil emissions are in the 
range from 11.3 to 21.9 kg CO₂e/ton.  
These findings present the significant variability in emissions associated with asphalt 
production, ranging from as low as 11.3 kg CO₂e/ton to as high as 83.1 kg CO₂e/ton, depending 
on the material composition and production processes.  The first group relies on virgin 
materials and conventional processes, leading to a higher CO₂e impact, while the second 
group includes more reclaimed asphalt, resulting in a much lower GWP.  
Conclusions and recommendations  
The emissions from the production of crushed stone vary significantly across the different 
crushing stages (Stages 0 –3). Emissions report ed in EPDs are not harmonized, as some EPDs 
combine emissions from multiple stages, exclude certain stages, or introduce additional 
stages for specific processes like washing (e.g., vaskeverk).  Furthermore, deviations in 
reporting have been observed, such as cases where later stages show lower emissions than 
earlier ones . These inconsistencies, along with insufficient detail in some EPDs, make it 
challenging to compare products accurately . For both exp erts and non -experts, interpreting 
the data presented in EPDs can be challenging, particularly when emissions are expressed 
across multiple stages,  referring to many  different product types  that  vary significantly 
between different producers.  
 
Norwegian Institute for Sustaina bility Research   Page 5 of 5 To address these issues, it is essential to standardize reporting practices by introducing clearer 
Product Category Rules (PCRs) for crushed stone EPDs. The PCRs should introduce  the 
accumulation of emissions across stages, ensuring that later stages include emissions from 
earlier ones . This should be clearly described in the EPD . The PCRs  should  also require  a clear 
separation and detailed description of each crushing stage to prevent emissions from being 
combined/aggregated into a single value. Additional processes, such as washing, should be 
transparently and clearly reported as separate stages.  Finally, a harmonized categorization of 
product types should be defined and used across all EPDs to enable the comparison of 
environmental performance betwee n products. Stricter standardized rules and requirements 
for performing EPDs will help to differentiate between variations due to methodological 
disparities and those due to real differences in performance of the products. In the EPDs for 
asphalt , a high  potential for emission reductions is found through higher reclaimed content.
